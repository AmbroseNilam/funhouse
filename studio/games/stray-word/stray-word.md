# Stray Word: the experience script

Four to ten people, about eight minutes a round. The host writes a fresh category and secret word (a
content request; if it fails, the game uses one of its own twenty-four). Code shows every player the
word except one, the Stray, who is only told what kind of thing it is. Each player says one word about
it, in an order the code enforces, for two rounds. Then everyone votes on who the Stray is.

What makes it a different game from the others: it is Leading Questions turned inside out (there the
host holds the word and one player steers; here everyone but one holds it and the odd one bluffs), and
the only game where the whole table speaks one word at a time, in a fixed order, under a one-word rule
the code checks.

## One round, as it plays

- Phones: a peek card (press and hold to read it). Insiders read "Your word: Kettle. It's a thing in a
  kitchen. One of you doesn't have it." The Stray reads "You're the stray. Everyone else knows the word.
  All you know is that it's a thing in a kitchen. Blend in." The TV says "Look at your phone."
- Host: "Everyone has the same secret word except one of you. Say one word about it, in turn, and don't
  make it too easy. It's a thing in a kitchen, and Spoon goes first. Two rounds." Short, then it stops.
- The order is code. Whoever is up holds to talk and says one word, or types it. Only that person has a
  live mic, so nobody can jump in. The TV shows who is up, a thirty-second clock and every word said,
  by round. Each phone shows "Said so far".
- The one-word rule is code. Filler is stripped ("um, it's, like, steam" is "steam"); more than one
  word is refused ("One word only"); a word already said is refused; and a word that is, contains or
  sits inside the secret is refused for anyone who knows it, with a note only on their own phone. The
  Stray's word is never refused for that reason, since they don't know it; if they happen to say the
  word, the table hears it.
- A speaker who stalls is skipped quietly after thirty seconds, and the TV moves on.
- After the last word of the last round the host calls the vote in one line. Everyone votes on their
  phone. The most votes, with no tie, is the accused. A tie, or the wrong person, and the Stray wins and
  the host reveals the word.
- A caught Stray gets one guess at the word, spoken or typed. Spoken, the host rules right or wrong
  against the real word; typed, the code checks it. Right and the Stray wins anyway.
- The reveal: the word on the TV, who the Stray was, and a dry line that makes the Stray look good
  whether they were caught or not.

## What the host is told, and never told

Told: the word, the category, and when to speak. Never told: who the Stray is. It speaks at the start,
at the start of round two, at the vote, at the guess and at the end; each word is a quiet fact that
never wakes it. It rules only after the Stray has spoken a guess; a ruling at any other moment is
dropped. A test deals the Stray to every seat and checks that the TV, the host and every other phone
see exactly the same thing whoever it is.

## Awkward cases

- Four players: the fewest. With three there are only two words to go on, so the box says four.
- Ten players: the most, since two rounds of ten words is already a long wait for the last speaker.
- Someone leaves: the Stray leaving ends the round with the word revealed; anyone else leaving takes
  them out of the order (and restarts the vote if it was open). Under four left ends the round.
- A speaker mumbles: refused as "One word only", no penalty, try again with a fresh box.
- The Stray says nothing when asked to guess: the clock ends it, the room wins.
- The room's captions show what was said aloud, so a secret blurted into the room is on the TV as
  caption text. Nothing can stop a person saying it; the game just refuses to count it as their word.

## What the app would need that does not exist yet

- The host cannot nudge a stalled speaker out loud; the TV just moves on.
- A phone control that lists the words said in a column rather than as one line of text.
