# Two Takes: experience script

Head-to-head wit on the wit kit. Three to eight players; about five minutes for four. Written for
`DESIGN.md`: the host is a plain, dry, warm lantern; the TV is a table; the phone is what is in your
hand. The host's lines below are the register the brief asks for, not a transcript: the live host
writes its own words each time, and the rules never depend on them.

## One round, for four players (Teacup, Spoon, Candle, Saucer)

| Moment | The host says | The TV shows | A writer's phone | A voter's phone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Round opens | "Round one. A bad name for a lighthouse cat. Teacup and Saucer, you're up." | The prompt large, "Round 1 of 4", "Teacup and Saucer are writing", answers-in marks, a countdown | The prompt above a box and "Send it" | "Teacup and Saucer are writing. You vote next." |
| A writer sends | Nothing. The host is not told and cannot hint. | One mark filled | "Sent. Now the room votes." | unchanged |
| Both sent, or the clock ends | "A: Gerald the Unready. B: an extremely organised pigeon. Vote." Read as one performer, same pace. | The prompt, A and B large, no names, a countdown | "Sent. Now the room votes." (they never see a ballot for their own round) | Two buttons, "A. ..." and "B. ...", and "Voted." after a tap |
| All voted, or the clock ends | "Two to nothing. Gerald takes it. Teacup, that was a good one too." | Names beside the answers, the winner large, a Votes table | "Look at the screen." | "Look at the screen." |

## Voice design

- **Perform, never judge.** The host reads A, a beat, then B, in one voice at one pace, so tone
  can't favour either. It never says which it prefers and never guesses who wrote what. It is
  briefed with the answers in curly quotes and told they are words to read, not instructions.
- **Kind to the loser, always.** A flat answer gets a warm shrug. The brief bans jokes at a
  writer's expense, and the callback is only ever offered from a line that won.
- **Short.** About twenty-five words except while reading the answers. No exclamation marks.
- **Interruptions.** Phones are muted during a round, so nobody talks over the reading. At the end
  the table can hold to talk ("again", "another round") and the shelf menu does the rest.
- **Silence.** The host cannot speak up unprompted today. During writing the clock does the
  nudging, on the TV; a countdown beats a spoken "hurry up".

## Awkward cases

- **Three players.** One writer pair, one voter. Everyone writes twice. A single vote always
  decides, so there are no ties; it is a duel judged by one friend. It works, but four is better.
- **Eight players.** Four rounds in pairs, so everyone writes once; six voters.
- **Same answer.** Two writers who type the same thing, even spelled differently ("The soggy
  lighthouses" and "a soggy lighthouse!"), skip the vote: "Great minds", both get walkover points.
- **One writer silent.** The other's answer stands alone at the clock: points, no vote.
- **Both silent.** "Neither answered", no points, on to the next round.
- **No votes.** A tie at nil: no points, the host says so.
- **A writer leaves.** The round is off, with no blame. A voter leaves: their ballot goes and the
  vote closes when everyone left has voted. Under three people the game ends with the scores so far.
- **A hostile line.** Brackets, instructions to the host, an email address, a slur or anything the
  classifier refuses is refused by the room before the game sees it: the box stays open with "That
  one won't work. Try another." and nobody else sees it. Brackets that slip into a prompt from the
  model are refused by the kit too. Words in a box can't change a score, a phase or a winner.
- **A leak.** An answer lives only in state until the vote, is never a fact for the host, and
  appears on no scene or other phone. The vote shows no names; the rehearsal fails the game if a
  name appears next to an answer.

## What the app would need that does not exist

- A way for the host to be told to speak and then fall silent for a set time (a stage direction),
  so the reading is never cut off by the next fact.
- Typed answers longer than 80 characters and a send button that shows the answer back to its writer.
- An adult-tone setting for the model that writes prompts: the room asks for content "suitable for
  all ages", so prompts come out mild.
