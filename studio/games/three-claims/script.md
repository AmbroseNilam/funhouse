# Three Claims: experience script

Two Truths and a Lie, with a host that interviews, on the claims kit. Three to eight players; about ten
minutes at six (a minute and a half of writing, then about ninety seconds a claimant). Written for
`DESIGN.md`: the host is a plain, dry, warm lantern; the TV is a table; the phone is what is in your
hand. The host's lines below are the register the brief asks for, not a transcript: the live host writes
its own words each time, and the rules never depend on them. The working name is unchecked against
trademark registers; the mechanic is a folk game.

## One turn, for four players (Teacup, Spoon, Candle, Saucer), Spoon up

| Moment | The host says | The TV shows | Spoon's phone | The others' phones |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Everyone writes | "Three claims each, on your phones. Make them ones you can talk about." | "Three Claims", "Everyone writes", a ready count and a countdown | A box, "Claim 1 of 3", then 2, then 3. A saved claim shows under the box. Then "Which one is the lie?" with the three claims to tap | The same, for their own claims |
| Spoon's turn opens | "Spoon, you're up. Where was the lighthouse?" (one question, about the claim the code chose) | "Spoon's three claims", the claims lettered A, B, C in a shuffled order, "Claimant 2 of 4", Spoon's place card lit | "Hold your phone to answer the host." | "Spoon is on the stand. Listen, and decide which claim is the lie." |
| Spoon answers | "And when was that?" (a second question, a different thing asked for) | unchanged, a countdown | Hold to talk. Only this phone is heard. | Muted |
| Spoon answers again | "Saucer, you have a question. Hold your phone and ask." | "Saucer has a question for Spoon", Saucer's place card lit | Muted: Spoon cannot talk over the question | Saucer: "Hold your phone and ask Spoon one question." The others wait |
| Saucer asks | "Spoon, your answer." | "Spoon is answering Saucer" | Hold to talk | Muted |
| Spoon answers | "Pick the lie on your phones." | "Which of Spoon's claims is the lie?", A, B, C, a votes-in count and a countdown | "The table is voting on you." | Three buttons: "A. ...", "B. ...", "C. ..."; "Locked in." after a tap |
| All voted, or the clock ends | "Claim B was the lie. Two of three fooled, so Spoon takes six. Candle caught it, and gets two." | "Claim B was the lie", the lie marked on the TV, "2 fooled, 1 caught it", a points table | "Look at the screen." | "Look at the screen." |
| After the last turn | "Candle wins on eleven. Everyone else, I'd want you at my table. Another round?" | Final scores, leaders marked | | |

Scoring: three points to the claimant for each person fooled, two to each person who caught the lie.
With three players the claimant faces two, and a sweep by either side doubles: both fooled, the
claimant takes twelve; both caught it, each takes four.

## Voice design

- **One question per reply.** The code tells the host exactly one thing to ask: which claim it is about
  and what to ask for, a detail, a date, a place or a name. It never asks for a reason, and the brief
  forbids health, sex, money, family and work. The code also turns down a claim that goes near those
  subjects when it is typed, so the host is never handed one to ask about.
- **It does not know the lie.** The code holds the mark. The host is told the lie only at the reveal, so
  its questions, its tone and its pauses carry nothing. The claim it asks about is dealt by the code from
  the same random source whatever the mark.
- **One speaker at a time.** The phase decides who may hold to talk: the claimant in their two answers,
  the named player in their question, the claimant again in the last answer. Everyone else is muted, and
  the room drops their audio, so the host cannot answer the wrong person.
- **Tally, not teasing the person.** At the reveal the host reads which claim was the lie and the numbers,
  and teases the result: "Nobody believed the pigeon." It never remarks on who lies well or badly.
- **Kind to a quiet claimant.** If the claimant says nothing, the clock moves the turn on; the host is
  not told they stayed quiet as a fault.
- **Interruptions.** Someone speaking out of turn is not heard at all, so the host stays quiet and does
  not reply to it. At the end the table can hold to talk ("again", "another round").
- **Silence.** The host cannot speak up unprompted today. The clocks do the nudging, on the TV.

## Awkward cases

- **Three players.** The claimant faces two. It works, and a sweep doubles so a lone claimant has a real
  swing. Four to six is better.
- **Eight players.** Eight turns of about ninety seconds is twelve minutes of interviews; the writing is
  the same. Anyone who finishes early waits, so the clock is two and a half minutes at the most.
- **A claim the filter refuses.** The box stays on the same claim and the TV and the host never see it.
- **A claim about health, money, family, work or romance, a repeat, or one with brackets.** The game turns
  it down with "Not that one" and the box stays on the same claim.
- **Someone is slow.** The table waits for the clock. A player who did not finish does not take a turn but
  still votes on everyone else.
- **Nobody finishes.** The game ends with "The table got small".
- **The claimant leaves.** The turn is off, nobody scores, the host says so without blame and without the lie.
- **The named player leaves.** The question is skipped and the claimant answers into the vote.
- **A voter leaves.** Their ballot goes; if theirs was the last one outstanding the vote closes.
- **Fewer than three left.** The game ends there.
- **The claimant confesses aloud.** Their words reach the host and the TV caption, because holding to
  talk is public. The game still scores by the votes; a confession just makes a short round.

## What the app would need that it does not have

- A colour on a scene block, so the claims could be drawn in the claimant's seat colour. Today the colour
  shows on the claimant's place card, which the turn lights.
- A way to hear a muted player so the host can say "wait your turn". Today muted audio is dropped.
