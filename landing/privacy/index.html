Funhouse privacy

There are no accounts. This page covers what Funhouse sends, who receives it and what is kept.

Your voice

Your phone asks for the mic the first time you talk. After that, the mic stays open on your phone. It keeps only the last fraction of a second and sends nothing until you talk.

Your phone sends sound from when you tap Talk until you tap Stop, go quiet, reach the time limit or leave the page. If you choose hold to talk, it sends sound for as long as you hold. It also sends a moment before and after, so your first and last words aren't cut off.

Your camera

The scanner uses your camera to read the code on the TV. Frames stay on your phone, and nothing is stored.

Who receives what

  • Meta turns what you say into text. That text, what you type, your name and what's happening in the game go to Meta's AI model so the host can reply.
  • Paintings. Lines written for a painting go to Meta's image model.
  • ElevenLabs turns the host's replies into speech, and those replies can include your name or words.
  • TypeSafe AI receives some typed answers and some of what you say. Its Jev model judges things like whether two answers mean the same.
  • Cloudflare carries the site's traffic.

Quiz topics and web search

When you say a topic for Quizmaster, it goes to Meta's AI model, which searches the web to help write the questions. Meta runs the search. We don't know which search services it uses, so your words may reach them. We ask Meta not to save the request as a stored response, but its usual terms below still apply.

The topic and its questions stay in our server's memory for up to an hour, even after your room closes. Another room choosing the same topic can get the same questions. So please don't say anything private as a topic.

Agents

Someone at the table can give a seat to their own AI agent with a link. That agent sees what that seat's phone would show and what's on the TV, which can include your name and what you've said. It's run by whoever invited it, under that service's terms, and we don't control what it keeps.

What those companies do with it

We send it from our account, and they keep it under their own terms.

  • Meta says it doesn't use content sent to the Standard models we use for Muse Spark to train its models. While we use Meta's free credits, Meta says content may be used for product improvement. Meta may check content for abuse and doesn't publish a fixed time it keeps it.
  • ElevenLabs keeps the text and audio by default and doesn't publish a fixed time for them. It doesn't use them for training, because we've opted out, though the opt-out doesn't cover anything sent before we turned it on.
  • TypeSafe says it doesn't train models on that text. It keeps data for as long as reasonably necessary, with no fixed time.
  • Cloudflare handles traffic for us and doesn't publish a fixed time for its logs.

What we keep

Your name, your room and what's said are held in the server's memory while the room is open. The app doesn't save them to disk. A room closes after five minutes with nobody in it, or fifteen minutes with no players. A restart clears everything.

Server logs

Our server keeps a technical log so we can fix problems. It records room codes, player numbers, timings and errors. It's written not to include what anyone said, what the host replied, or names. We haven't set a fixed time for deleting it.

If a connection fails, the software linking our server to Cloudflare can log the web address. For an agent link, that address includes the seat's key.

On your phone

Your phone remembers your name, room code and seat so you can rejoin. It also remembers which gestures you've learned and your settings.

Cookies and analytics

The app has no cookies, ads or analytics.
