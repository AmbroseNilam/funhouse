Funhouse privacy

Your voice. Your phone only sends sound while you talk: from when you tap Talk until you tap Stop, go quiet, reach the time limit or leave the page, or for as long as you press and hold if you choose that. It also sends a moment just before and just after so your first and last words aren't cut off. The rest of the time, the mic stays open on your phone, keeps only the last fraction of a second, and sends nothing.

Your camera. The scanner uses your camera to read the code on the TV. It's processed on your phone and nothing is stored.

Who handles it. What you say is turned into text by Meta. That text, your name and what's happening in the game go to Meta's AI model so the host can reply. The host's replies, which can include your name or words, are turned into speech by ElevenLabs, or by OpenAI if ElevenLabs is unavailable. Cloudflare carries the site's traffic.

Quick judgments. Some typed answers, and some of what you say in rooms trying this, go to TypeSafe AI, whose Jev model judges things like whether two answers mean the same. TypeSafe's privacy policy says it doesn't train models on that text and keeps data for as long as reasonably necessary, with no fixed time.

Quiz topics and web search. When you say a topic for Quizmaster, that topic goes to Meta's AI model, which searches the web to help write the questions. Meta runs the search, and we don't know which search services it uses, so your words may reach them. We ask Meta not to save the request as a stored response, but its usual terms below still apply. The topic and its questions stay in our server's memory for up to an hour, even after your room closes, and another room choosing the same topic can get the same questions. So please don't say anything private as a topic.

How long those companies keep it. We send it from our account, and they keep it under their own terms:

  • Meta says it doesn't use content sent to the Standard models we use for Muse Spark to train its models. While we use Meta's free credits, Meta says content may be used for product improvement. Meta may check it for abuse, and doesn't publish a fixed time it keeps it.
  • ElevenLabs keeps the text and audio by default and doesn't publish a fixed time for them. It may use it to train its models.
  • OpenAI keeps speech requests for up to 30 days to check for abuse and doesn't use them for training.
  • Cloudflare handles traffic for us and doesn't publish a fixed time for its logs.

Agents. Someone at the table can give a seat to their own AI agent with a link. That agent sees what that seat's phone would show and what's on the TV, which can include your name and what you've said. It's run by whoever invited it, under that service's terms, and we don't control what it keeps.

What we keep. Your name, room and what's said are held in the server's memory while the room is open. The app doesn't save them to disk. A room closes after five minutes with nobody in it, or fifteen minutes with no players, and a restart clears everything.

Logs. Logs record things like room codes, player numbers and timings. They don't hold what anyone said, what the host replied, or names.

On your phone. Your phone remembers your name, room code and seat so you can rejoin, which gestures you've learned, and your settings.

The app has no accounts, cookies, ads or analytics.
